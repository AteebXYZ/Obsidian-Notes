## Angular Displacement and Speed

Angular displacement is given by:
$$
\Delta\theta = \frac{\text{distance travelled around the cirlce}}{\text{radius of the circle}}
$$

Angular displacement is the same as the length of an arc in a circle equation:
$$
S = r\theta 
$$
$$
\Delta\theta  = \frac{S}{r}
$$
 Where:
	S is arc length
	 r is radius of the circle

Angular speed is defined as:
$$
\omega = \frac{\Delta \theta}{\Delta t} = \frac{2\pi}{T} = 2\pi f \text{ where, } f = \frac{1}{T}
$$
If object velocity is constant or linear, angular velocity is:
$$
\omega = \frac{v}{r} = \frac{\Delta\theta}{\Delta t}
$$
## Centripetal Acceleration

Always perpendicular to direction of travel or direction of linear velocity and directed toward the center of the circular path.

Even though the velocity remains constant in magnitude, since the direction changes, velocity is also constantly changing, therefore, the object has centripetal acceleration.

#### Calculating Centripetal Acceleration:

Centripetal acceleration is defined by:
$$
a = \frac{v^{2}}{r}
$$
Where
	a = centripetal acceleration
	v = linear speed of object
	r = radius of circular path
$$
\omega = \frac{v}{r} \therefore v = \omega r
$$
So substituting ${}v = \omega r{}$ into the equation for centripetal acceleration we get:
$$

 a = \frac{(\omega r)^{2}}{r}
\implies a = \frac{\omega^{2}r^{2}}{r} 
\implies a = \frac{\omega^{2}\cancel{ r }^{2}}{\cancel{ r }} \therefore a = r\omega^{2}
$$
Where
	${}\omega{}$ = angular speed

## Centripetal Force

Centripetal Force is defined by:
$$
F = \frac{mv^{2}}{r}
$$
Where:
	F = C.Force
	m = mass of object
	v = linear velocity of object
	r = radius of circular path

Again, since: ${}v = r\omega{}$:

$$
\begin{gathered}
&F = \frac{m(r\omega)^{2}}{r} \implies \frac{mr^{2}\omega^{2}}{r}\implies \frac{m\cancel{ r }^{2}\omega^{2}}{\cancel{ r }} \implies F = mr\omega^{2}  \\
&\text{The Femboy Equation, mrw}
\end{gathered}
