
We do the reciprocal of the ${}\ln{}$ function, then multiply it by the ${}\frac{dy}{dx}{}$ of the inside of the ${}\ln{}$ function
$$
\begin{gather}
&y = \ln (ax+b) \\ \\
&\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{ax+b}\times a
\end{gather}
$$

More examples:
$$
\begin{gather}
& y=\ln 2x  \\
& {\color{cyan}\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{2x}\times x \implies \frac{1}{x}}
\end{gather}
$$



$$
\begin{gather}
& y = \ln x^{2} \\
& {\color{cyan} \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{x^{2}} \times 2x\implies \frac{2}{x}}
\end{gather}
$$


$$
\begin{gather}
 
& y = \ln\left( \frac{x-1}{x+2} \right)  \\ \\
& y = \ln(x-1) - \ln(x+2) \\
& {\color{cyan}\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{x-1}-\frac{1}{x+2}}
\end{gather}
$$



$$
\begin{gather}
&y = \ln x^{2} \\
&\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{2x}{^{2}} \implies {\color{cyan}\frac{2}{x}}
\end{gather}
$$


$$
\begin{gather}
&y=(\ln x)^{2} \\
& \frac{dy}{dx} = 2(\ln x)\times \frac{1}{x} \\
&  \\
& {\color{cyan}\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{2\ln x}{x}}
\end{gather}
$$

Finding ${}x{}$:
$$
\begin{gather}
&y=x^{2}\ln x  \\
& \\
&u=x^{2} \\
&u'=2x \\
& v=\ln x \\
& v'=\frac{1}{x} \\
&  \\
& \frac{dy}{dx} = 2x\ln x+x^{2}\times \frac{1}{x} \implies 2x\ln x+x \\
&  \\
& 0=x(2\ln x+1) \\
& {\color{cyan}x=0} \\
& {\color{cyan}x=e^{-1/2}}
\end{gather}
$$
